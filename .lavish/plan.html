What we are building, in plain English

After people vote in Nigeria, the results are written on a paper sheet and stuck on the wall at the polling unit. People take photos of it. Days later, when the final numbers are announced, nobody can easily check if they match what was on the wall.

We are building a website that works on a phone. A person takes a photo of the result sheet, and the site does four things:

  1. Keeps the photo safe and stamps it with a digital fingerprint, so anyone can tell later if the photo was changed.
  2. Reads the numbers off the sheet automatically and checks the maths (do the votes add up?).
  3. Compares photos from different people. If two people photographed the same sheet and the numbers differ, it flags it for a human to look at.
  4. Shows everything publicly on a simple page: the photo, the numbers, a clear status and a history of what happened.

It does not replace INEC and it never says who won or accuses anyone of cheating. A mismatch just means "someone should take a look." It also works with a weak internet connection: the photo is saved on the phone and sent when the signal returns.

Everyone can use it. There is a large-text, high-contrast mode, short plain-English and Pidgin explanations of each status, and a "Listen" button that reads a result sheet aloud, for people who can't easily read it.

7Integrity design, in one screen

Evidence record

submissions
  id, polling_unit_id, submitter_id
  source_type   official_public | independent | demo_synthetic
  image_path, sha256_client, sha256_server
  captured_at, received_at, synced_at
extractions       -- machine-assisted, never edited in place
  submission_id, fields jsonb, confidence jsonb
verification_results
  submission_id, checks jsonb, status
reviews           -- human decisions, append-only
audit_events      -- append-only, hash-chained
  id, type, subject_id, payload,
  prev_hash, hash, created_at

Status labels (neutral wording)

Pending reviewNeeds human reviewCorroboratedDisputed

Corroborated means two or more independent submitters agree on every extracted field. Disputed means they differ, which is "a reason to look", never an accusation. No status says "fraud" or "winner".

Hash honesty, built into the UI

A tooltip next to every fingerprint: "This proves the file hasn't changed since it was captured. It does not prove the document was genuine." That is the blueprint's own safeguard, and it reads as maturity to judges.
