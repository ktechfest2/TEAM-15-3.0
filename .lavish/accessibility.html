Making voting more accessible: what could we do?

The organisers list this as one of the example areas: "making voting more accessible for elderly voters and people with disabilities." Here are the ideas, honestly scored against our 12.5-hour clock and the VOTE-GUARD 360 core we locked.

9 ideas2 ways to use themNothing built yet

The short version

Many people can't easily read a printed result sheet: blind or low-vision voters, people who can't read English, and elderly voters who struggle with small print or complicated screens.

My suggestion: keep VOTE-GUARD as the main product, and add a small "Access layer" on top, built from three of these ideas. Its star feature is "Listen to this result sheet": the app reads the extracted numbers aloud in plain language. It reuses the OCR we're already building, so it costs little and it is a genuinely fresh idea.

Why it works for us

  • It reuses the OCR output we already build: no new data pipeline.
  • The reading is generated from the verified fields and status, so it never says more than the evidence supports ("two people agree", not "this is correct").
  • Browsers have built-in text-to-speech. English and Pidgin-friendly English are workable; Hausa, Yorùbá and Igbo voices are weak, so I'd show text translations and be honest about audio limits rather than fake it.
  • Translations need a native speaker to check them. Ask a teammate or friend; don't trust machine output blindly.

3Two ways to use these

Recommended

A · Access layer on VOTE-GUARD

Keep the plan as is, add A1 + A2 + A3 (about 3.5 hours across lanes C and D, mostly in the 05:30 robustness slot). Optional A4 if we're ahead.

Gain: answers the brief's "voters who don't read English, basic phones, community leaders" line and gives a memorable demo moment. Innovation and relevance both rise.

Cost: small. Nothing in the Must list moves.

Alternative

B · Pivot to accessibility as the main product

Make ACCESS-VOTE the core: accessible polling-unit directory, barrier reports, rights card, listen and easy-read features.

Gain: directly on one of the brief's example areas and very human.

Cost: the integrity engine, which is our 25% technical-execution story, goes. With the lanes already planned and ~12 hours left, I'd advise against a pivot.
